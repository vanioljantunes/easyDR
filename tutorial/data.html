The table

Long form, one row per exposure category per study. Nothing has to be reshaped, sorted or aggregated, and the column names are guessed when you leave them out. Two bundled tables show the shape to copy.

ColumnWhat it holds
studlabThe study label. Rows sharing one are one study.
doseThe numeric dose you assigned to that category. Never inferred from a category range.
refOptional. Marks each study's reference category. When absent one is inferred and the guesses are printed.
designOptional. Case-control, incidence rate, or cumulative incidence. Each study keeps its own.
The dose is the one judgement nobody can make for you.

A published table reports a category, say 10 to 20 g/day, not a number. Turning that into a dose is a decision about the midpoint, the median intake, or whatever the authors reported, and it changes the slope. easyDR will not guess it, and the book's extraction chapter is mostly about making that decision defensibly.

sm decides which columns are read

smOutcomeColumns read
"RR"risk ratioevent.e, n.e   or   TE, lower, upper
"OR"odds ratio, from a case-control designevent.e, n.e   or   TE, lower, upper
"IRR"incidence rate ratioevent.e, with person-time in n.e
"MD"mean differencemean.e, sd.e, n.e
"SMD"standardised mean differencemean.e, sd.e, n.e

Passing a column that belongs to the other kind of outcome is an error rather than a name quietly ignored, because a fit that looks like it used your standard deviations and did not is the worst of both. 

easydr(alcohol_cancer, sm = "IRR", shape = "linear",
       sd.e = "sd")
#> Error: easydr: sm = "IRR" is a binary outcome,
#>   so sd.e is not read. Drop it or change sm.

When the table has no counts

Both covariance reconstructions, Greenland-Longnecker and Hamling, need denominators. A table carrying only an effect with its limits supports neither, so the contrasts are treated as independent and the printed output says so. That understates the correlation, so supply the counts whenever the paper gives them.

The extraction sheet that produces a table in this shape is chapter 2 of the book.
