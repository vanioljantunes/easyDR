One call, and seven things to do with it

Dose-response meta-analysis argued the way the meta package argues

1

There is no formula argument

What a dose-response usually asks of you

# reshape, compute the log ratio, compute its
# standard error, then describe the model as
# a formula over columns you just made
fit <- dosresmeta(formula = logrr ~ dose,
                  type = type, id = id, se = se,
                  cases = cases, n = peryears,
                  data = d)

Every one of those columns is something you had to derive first, and logrr ~ dose is a sentence about your spreadsheet rather than about your review.

What easyDR asks

# name the measure the studies report, and
# the shape you decided on
fit <- easydr(alcohol_cancer, sm = "IRR",
               shape = "linear",
               dose.unit = "g/day",
               at = c(10, 25, 50))
sm decides everything downstream.

It says which columns are read, which covariance approximation is available, and what scale every reported number comes back on. The contrasts, the standard errors and the within-study covariances are derived for you, from the table you already extracted.
2

The family

One function fits. The other seven take what it returned. Nothing else is exported, and nothing in the package asks you to call a second thing to get the first answer.

FunctionWhat it gives youWhat it will not do
easydr() The pooled dose-response, its heterogeneity, the prediction interval, the predicted effects, the viability check and a name for the curve. Choose the shape. shape has no default.
easydr_select() Around forty candidate shapes, ranked, with the ties shown and the straight line among them. Adopt the winner. It calls the result a proposal and leaves the decision with you.
easydr_forest() One row per study: its own trend, its weight, and the pooled diamond underneath. Invent rows for a one-stage fit, which never produces per-study estimates.
easydr_curve() The fitted curve with its confidence band, the reported doses marked, the prediction band on request. Hide that a dose sits beyond the observed range.
easydr_loo() The fit with each study left out in turn, ranked by how far the result moved. Attach a p value. The refits are not independent.
easydr_metareg() A study characteristic regressed on the dose-response, with the between-moderator test and the residual heterogeneity. Call the association causal.
easydr_bubble() That regression drawn: study trends against the moderator, bubble area the weight. Fit its own line. The line is the regression's.
easydr_funnel() The study trends against their standard errors, with Egger's regression test for asymmetry. Say the asymmetry is publication bias.
3

Two checks you no longer have to remember

A dose-response review goes wrong twice. Before the model, because the included studies never could have supported one. After it, because the curve gets described in words the literature does not use.

Both checks run inside easydr() and both appear in the output. The viability check reads the prepared analysis frame, so it reports the reference category the fit actually used rather than guessing that a zero dose is one. 

fit <- easydr(alcohol_cancer, sm = "IRR",
               shape = "linear")

fit$viability   # the per-study table
fit$curve_label # "positive association"

Defaults applied: - intake: reported effect with limits, counts used for the covariance
Estimation is delegated to dosresmeta. easyDR prepares the data, calls it once, and reports the answer in the words a systematic reviewer uses.
