What to do with the fit

Five functions, all taking the object easydr() returned

1

easydr_forest()

easydr_forest(fit)

# and for a curve, where no single trend exists,
# the rows become one dose
easydr_forest(rcs, at = 25)

A dose-response study contributes a trend, not an effect, so that is what a row shows: the study's own slope from the two-stage first stage. These are the engine's own estimates, not something recomputed for the picture.

A one-stage fit has no rows.

It estimates every study inside a single model and never produces a per-study coefficient. Rather than draw the pooled line eight times, this refuses and says to refit with proc = "2stage".

3

easydr_bubble() and easydr_funnel()

The bubble line is the regression's own.

The bubbles are the unadjusted study trends and the line comes from easydr_metareg(), not from a fresh regression through the points. That is why a study can sit off the line: the distance is the residual heterogeneity the printed output quantifies.
Egger, not Deeks.

Deeks' funnel asymmetry test is for diagnostic test accuracy, where the effective sample size is the sensible axis, and it does not apply here. A dose-response review has one trend per study with a standard error, which is the setting Egger's regression was written for.

With eight studies the test has little power, so the plot says so in red rather than leaving it to be remembered. Asymmetry has several causes and publication bias is only one.
Every one of these returns the table it drew, so the numbers behind a picture can be checked against the fit they came from.
